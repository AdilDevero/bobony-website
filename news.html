Season 4 — New Horizon

Fourteen weeks of work, three internal test weekends, and one very tired development team later: the city is open. Here is what changed.

Economy

  • Vehicle prices rebalanced against a new inflation index; every class moved, most downward for starter cars.
  • Reputation is now shared across related jobs, so a good mechanic is also a known mechanic.
  • Business ledgers export to the phone, and the DA can request them with a warrant.

Institutions

  • The courthouse has interiors. Cases are scheduled, not improvised in a parking lot.
  • Internal Affairs received its own case queue and anonymous complaint form.
  • EMS gained tiered treatment items and revive gating; triage is a scene again.

Technical

  • Persistence layer rewritten; item loss on reconnect is down to zero in testing.
  • NUI bundle cut by 38%, which is most of why the harbour no longer stutters.
  • Queue system now shows your position and estimated wait.

Patch 4.2 notes

  • Phone: contracts, IDs and business documents persist across sessions.
  • Chop shop: clean VIN payouts reduced 20%, rare part drops increased.
  • Prison: workshop jobs now grant trade XP; escape cooldown fixed.
  • MDT: warrant history now searchable by plate and by officer.
  • Housing: fixed furniture desync for apartments above floor nine.
  • Fixed a duplication exploit in the trunk transfer flow — reported by a player, rewarded in game.

The harbour strike

It started with a single refusal. A driver for a player-run logistics company arrived at the docks, saw the picket line his own boss had ignored, and parked the truck.

Within forty minutes, three businesses had closed in solidarity, a union spokesperson had appeared on the in-game newspaper's live feed, and the LSPD had sent a lieutenant to negotiate in a parking lot because the harbour office was, technically, closed. Nobody scripted it. Nobody had to.

This is the thing we keep saying about serious roleplay: the best scenes are not content. They are consequences.
